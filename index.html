Music

Welcome To my Music Opinions!

Dark Side of the Moon - Pink Floyd

Dark Side of the Moon was one of the first Pink Floyd albums I was introduced to, after Division Bell, and it is also one of the most popular Pink Floyd albums to date, for a good reason.

Dark Side of the Moon contains 9 songs that blend together so perfectly it can be difficult to tell when a new song is playing. The psychedelic rock style adds sounds that you normally don't hear in songs like a cash register opening or clocks chiming, along with electrical sound effects. The album goes into themes of death, mental illness, greed, and conflict.
Overall Rating: 10/10 Absolutely recommended

Animals - Pink Floyd

Animals is another incredible Pink Floyd album, and another one of my personal favorites, with the musical styles and the shockingly long “Dogs”

“A lyrical departure from Pink Floyd's previous albums, Animals is a concept album that focuses on the sociopolitical conditions of mid-1970s Britain.”(Wikipedia) Animals also takes a loose basing on George Orwell’s Animal Farm, talking about classes of society like animals, as a critique of capitalism, (as opposed to Orewell’s stalinism critique).
Animals only has 5 songs, compared to the 9 of Dark Side of the Moon, with the most notable of which being
“Dogs”, sitting at a solid 17 minutes, concentrating on the aggressive world of business, "club tie and a firm handshake, a certain look in the eye and an easy smile", however behind the mask is ready "to pick out the easy meat...to strike when the moment is right". The song continues on to talk about aging and the emptiness of his existence after retiring rich but lonely, "just another sad old man, all alone and dying of cancer".
Sheep, the fourth song, takes on the perspective of the working class rebelling against the upper class, the “Dogs” and “Pigs”, and killing all the dogs and retiring to their homes. “Have you heard the news? The dogs are dead, You better stay home and do as you're told, Get out of the road if you wanna grow old”


Wish You Were Here - Pink Floyd

Wish You Were Here is another 5 song album of Pink Floyd’s, with some incredible songs, notably being Shine On You Crazy Diamond(parts 1-9 totalling to 25 minutes total) and Wish You Were Here, my personal two favorite songs due to their depth and the meaning behind them.

Shine On You Crazy Diamond opens and closes the album being split into two chunks, parts 1-5 and 6-9. The song is written for and dedicated to the founding Pink Floyd member Syd Barrett who left the band due to substance abuse and mental illness in 1968. He was slowly eased out of the band due to his LSD addiction making his ability to perform musically and interact with the band decline. He was replaced with his childhood friend David Gilmore.
Welcome to the Machine is another notable song in the album, due to its apparent lack of traditional instruments, with the bulk of the sound coming from a synthesizer, and some acoustic guitar. The song talks about the bands “disillusionment with the music industry as a money-making machine rather than a forum of artistic expression”(unknown source).
Have a Cigar follows along the same lines as Welcome to the Machine, scorning the industry “fat-cats” who just want the possible money from signing a band, with one company asking the band "by the way, which one's Pink?”(Wikipedia) on at least one occasion, clearly stating that the company was not actual fans and were just in it for the money.
Wish You Were Here once again references Barrett’s condition and the “dichotomy of Waters' character, with greed and ambition battling with compassion and idealism.”(Wikipedia). The album ends off with parts 6-9 of Shine On You Crazy Diamond to wrap it up nicely.
Overall Rating: 10/10 Incredible album that goes into Pink Floyd’s history and past experiences

Division Bell - Pink Floyd

Division Bell was the first Pink Floyd album I was introduced to, and despite it not being my favorite it still is an incredible album with quite an interesting story and a solid 11 songs total.

Division Bell is the second album recorded without Roger Waters, a key founding member, with the album being written mostly by David Gilmour the guitarist and singer, with Richard Wright, the keyboardist, his first lead vocal position since The Dark Side of the Moon. Gilmour’s Fiancėe co-wrote many lyrics as well, with many of them dealing with themes of communication. It was also the last album to be recorded with Wright, who died in 2008 (Wikipedia). After about two weeks spent doing improvisation, the band ended up with about 65 songs. The band voted on each track knocking it down to 27 and then from there to 11.
The album starts with the song cluster one, then moves on to What Do You Want from Me, which is described by Gilmour as a "straight Chicago blues tune", with the song originally having to do with personal relationships, however it drifted to a wider territory.
It then shifts into Poles Apart, with the lyrics mentioning former band member Syd Barrett in the first verse and Roger Waters in the second. It then moves to Marooned(instrumental) then A Great Day for Freedom, which addresses the hopes following the fall of the Berlin Wall and the following disappointment. It then moves onto Wearing the Inside Out, which is the only song on which David Gilmour does not get writing credit. Then it's Take It Back, about Mother Earth and how eventually she will go into self protection mode and extinct the human race to “Take Back” the planet(songfacts.com). Next is Coming Back to Life, the only song credited solely to David Gilmour, then its Keep Talking, which features samples of Stephen Hawking’s electronic voice taken from a tv advertisement. “Gilmour chose to use the speech after crying to the commercial, which he described as "the most powerful piece of television advertising that I’ve ever seen in my life.”(Wikipedia). Lost For Words is the “penultimate” track penned by Gilmour and his spouse Polly Samson, with the lyrics being a sarcastic and bitter reflection on “Gilmours then-strained relationship with former bandmate Roger Waters”(Wikipedia). High Hopes is the 11th and final song, with the lyrics referring to the early days of the band in Cambridge before they started making music. It also refers to ex-bandmate Syd Barrett, and speaks about the things one could have gained and lost in life.

Overall Rating: 10/10, An incredible album with quite a few interesting perspectives, with an incredable story behind it.


The Final Cut - Pink Floyd

The Final Cut takes a different vein then most other of Pink Floyd’s albums with a much darker and depressing take on things, specifically World War II and the “betrayal” of those who died in the war.

The Final Cut is the last album made with founder Roger Waters, who left in ‘85, and also the only album without founder Richard Wright, who left after The Wall due to pressure from Waters.
The Final Cut is an anti-war concept album that explores the “betrayal of fallen British servicemen”, such as Waters’s father, in hopes of the post-war dream, that victory would lead to a more peaceful world.
The album starts with The Post War Dream, featuring a radio being tuned to different stations discussing fallout shelters and the movement of ship construction being moved to Japan, with lyrics like “If it wasn't for the Nips Being so good at building ships” and “The yards would still be open on the Clyde”, Clyde referencing the River Clyde and its shipyards. It then goes on to mention the failed post-war dream "What happened to the post war dream?", while calling out Margaret Thatcher, ex Prime Minister of the UK for failing them with lines like “What have we done, Maggie, what have we done? What have we done to England?”
Your Possible Pasts starts by alluding to the Holocaust with the line "cattle trucks lying in wait for the next time" to reference the vehicles used in the Holocaust, and I feel altho the line “Some brighteyed and crazy, some frightened and lost” alludes to the same, with the expressions you would see on the faces of some of its victims. “Of their possible future, to take care” serves as a warning for the future, to not repeat past mistakes.
One Of The Few describes a veteran’s return home from the war “(specifically a pilot from the Battle of Britain, commonly known as The Few)” (Wikipedia), and pursuing a career as a teacher. “The title, "One of the Few", is a reference to Winston Churchill’s speech before the House of Commons on August 20, 1940 in which he stated “Never was so much owed by so many to so few” in reference to the Royal Air Force's defence of Great Britain.” (Wikipedia)
When The Tigers Broke Free tells the story of how Waters lost his father, Eric, in the war. It describes Operation Shingle in which both American and British troops landed on the beaches of Anzio, Italy, with the intention of liberating Rome from the germans. The forces included Z Company of the 8th Battalion and the Royal Fusiliers, in which Eric Served. The forward commander had asked to retreat back from a german Tiger I tank assault but the generals refused using them as a bait or a distraction “As the other rank s held back the enemy tanks for a while”
It then goes on to the second verse, that talks about the condolence letter from the British government “And kind old King George sent mother a note/When he heard that father was gone/It was, I recall, in the form of a scroll/With gold leaf adorned/His Majesty signed with his own rubber stamp”. The song closes out with the lines “When the tigers broke free/And no one survived from the Royal Fusiliers Company Z/They were all left behind, most of them dead/The rest of them dying/And that's how the high command took my daddy from me”
The Hero’s Return was written about a homecoming soldier who needs understanding but gets adulation. How he is thinking about the war despite having gone home, and how he is suffering from trauma and is missing the friend he lost, which is shown with the lines “Of the gunner's dying words/On the intercom”.
The Gunner’s Dream tells the story and thoughts of an airman gunner as he falls to his death in a raid, thinking about a safe world in the future without war. One could infer that this is the gunner mansion in The Hero’s Return. The gunner says “in the space between the heavens/And the corner of some foreign field/I had a dream” and says goodbye to his loved ones “Goodbye Max, goodbye Ma” while thinking of what his funeral service may be like with lines like “After the service, when you're walking slowly to the car/And the silver in her hair shines in the cold November air/You hear the tolling bell and touch the silk in your lapel/And as the teardrops rise to meet the comfort of the band”, then starts talking about his hopes for the future with lines like “A place to stay, enough to eat/Somewhere, old heroes shuffle safely down the street” and “You can relax on both sides of the tracks/And maniacs don't blow holes in bandsmen by remote control”, likely referencing the Hyde Park and Regent’s Park bombings. The song closes with the lines “In the corner of some foreign field/The gunner sleeps tonight/What's done is done” as a form of closure, with the final lines being “We cannot just write off his final scene/Take heed of the dream/Take heed”
Paranoid Eyes delves into themes of isolation and disillusionment, mentioning that people present facades to the world and often disguise their true sleeves to avoid vulnerability. “Button your lip and don't let the shield slip” implies a lack of expression or of suppression of self to stay hidden or go unnoticed. “And if they try to break down your disguise with their questions You can hide, hide, hide behind paranoid eyes” references isolation, often self-imposed, using hiding as a method to avoid vulnerability. It is also said that the song follows the teachers fall into alcoholism, with lines like “Fixing your grin as you casually lean on the bar”, but more specifically “Now you're lost in a haze of alcohol soft middle age”, showing the teacher hiding behind a mask to avoid questions and vulnerability, likely about the war, hiding those things “Behind brown and mild eyes”
Get Your Filthy Hands Off My Desert is about the wars in the middle east and the desert areas like Afghanistan and Beirut using desert instead of dessert as a way of saying to not touch their land. “Galtieri took the Union Jack/And Maggie, over lunch one day/Took a cruiser with all hands/Apparently to make him give it back”
The Fletcher Memorial Home “is about Waters' frustration with the leadership of the world since World War II”(Wikipedia) suggesting the “colonial waster of life and limb” be put into a specially funded retirement home, while also calling the leadership “overgrown infants” and “incurable tyrants” suggesting that they are unable to understand anything other than violence and their own faces on a TV screen. Calling the home the “Fletcher Memorial Home” is in honor of Roger Waters' father Eric Fletcher Waters. The song calls the leaders “Incurable Tyrants and Kings” and “Colonial Wasters of Life and Limb” , ending the song with the suggestion to “Are you having a nice time? (Goodbye!)/Now the final solution can be applied” as a reference to Hitlers “Final Solution” of executing the leaders.
Southampton Dock talks about the Southampton Dock from which many soldiers would depart for the war. “The song describes a woman who "bravely waves" the soldiers "Goodbye again ""(Wikipedia) as a lament to the returning war heroes and those who didn't make it back. Shown with lines like “There were too many spaces in the line”. “She bravely waves the boys goodbye again” “A mute reminder of the poppy fields and graves” “We spent what they had made/But in the bottom of our hearts/We felt the final cut”
The Final Cut “tells of a man's isolation, depression, sexual repression and rejection. At the end of the song he attempts suicide but "never had the nerve to make the final cut””(Wikipdeia). The song talks about the man's dark side and how he worries that he will be treated differently if he shows it, with lines like “And if I show you my dark side /Will you still hold me tonight? /And if I open my heart to you/And show you my weak side /What would you do?” and then talks about a failed suicide attempt. “I held the blade in trembling hands /Prepared to make it but just then the phone rang /I never had the nerve to make the final cut”.
Not Now John is the only song to feature Gilmour as the lead vocals, and is one of the only Pink Floyd tracks with strong profanity.
Two Suns In The Sunset portrays a nuclear holocaust as a result of a world obsessed with war and fighting. “It's day again/The sun is in the east/Even though the day is done/Two suns in the sunset/Could be the human race is run?” talking about a nuclear mushroom cloud in the east as a second sun, and mentions the things left undone “I think of all the good things/That we have left undone”. The album ends with the lines “Finally I understand the feelings of the few/Ashes and diamonds/Foe and friend/We were all equal in the end”.

Overall Rating: 10/10, some incredible themes and great story however there are definitely some better albums.
